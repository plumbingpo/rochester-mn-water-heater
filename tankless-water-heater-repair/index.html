Tankless units are sophisticated machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Rochester, MN homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Rochester home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. By the time we recommend a repair, we know it is the right one.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. A mistake with either one is not a small mistake.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Descaling is done with a pump and service valves, not by pouring vinegar into an inlet and hoping. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
