Keeping Your Eyota Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
