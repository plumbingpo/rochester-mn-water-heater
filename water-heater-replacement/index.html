How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Age is the first clue. A tank past eight to twelve years has done most of its work, and the serial number on the label tells us exactly how old yours is. The symptoms come next. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and homeowners in Rochester, MN usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

A replacement is the perfect moment to fix whatever the last heater got wrong. Rochester Water Heater sizes the new unit to your household, not just to the label on the old one. For tanks, that means looking at the first-hour rating, the amount of hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour, rather than tank gallons alone. For tankless units, it means sizing to flow rate in gallons per minute and to the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. We walk you through the real trade-offs between gas and electric, tank and tankless, and standard and heat pump models, in plain language and without pressure, and we check whether your gas line, venting, and electrical service can support the choice before you commit.

The swap itself follows a checklist, not a guess: the old tank drained and hauled away, new connectors and a working shutoff installed, a sediment trap added and the gas line leak-tested, venting sized and pitched to the manufacturer's spec, and the relief valve piped to a safe discharge point. Where local code calls for it, we add a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps, and we pull the permit when one is required. Before we leave, we fill and purge the tank, confirm draft and check for carbon monoxide on gas units, set the temperature near 120 degrees, and walk you through the new heater. When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Consider the vent first. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
