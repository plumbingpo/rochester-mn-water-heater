No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Here in Rochester, MN, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Rochester Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Rochester call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and replacing them one by one is slow and costly.

Our technicians approach gas heaters with instruments rather than assumptions. They measure thermocouple millivolts, read gas pressure at the valve, test draft at the hood, check for CO around the appliance, and leak-test every joint they touch. That process fixes the actual fault the first time and leaves your home safer than before. We also give you an honest assessment of the heater itself. A tank past about ten or twelve years with a failing valve or a leak is usually a replacement, and we will say so before you spend money on a repair.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
